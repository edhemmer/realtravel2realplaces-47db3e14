# RT2RP Experience Rebuild

## Standard

RT2RP must feel like a Travel Operating System, not a set of web pages.

The app experience is built around one spine:

1. Today: what matters now, what changed, what to do next.
2. Timeline: the complete trip in order.
3. Travel: movement, routes, airport timing, transit, driving mode.
4. Places: nearby food, services, airport/local context.
5. Records: reservations, receipts, documents, travelers, reports.

Every screen should answer at least one of these questions:

- What do I need to do next?
- What changed?
- Where do I need to be?
- What do I need to know before moving?
- What is safely stored here so I do not need another app or email search?

## Visual System

The visual system comes from the RT2RP logo only:

- Deep navy / obsidian base.
- Gold / champagne premium accents.
- Controlled signal cyan for live status and primary actions.
- Compass, route pin, globe, and fly / drive / train transport language.

Avoid unrelated illustrations, decorative graphics, rainbow gradients, novelty colors, and generic travel art.

Icons are allowed when they are functional UI controls or directly aligned to travel operations.

## Product Rhythm

The product should behave like one app:

- Fewer primary nav choices.
- Clear next action first.
- Timeline always available.
- Movement and live context connected.
- Provider status visible when data is live, cached, missing, or unavailable.
- No silent mock data. If data is unavailable, show why.

## Research Signals

High-performing travel apps emphasize one itinerary, one map/context view, easy reservation intake, route visibility, collaboration, expenses, packing, and offline usefulness. RT2RP should go further by becoming the operational layer after booking: the user may book anywhere, then RT2RP manages the trip.

## Acceptance Gate

A change is not good enough if it only makes a tab prettier.

A change is good enough when the user can open the app under pressure and quickly understand:

- The current trip state.
- The next move.
- The risk or alert state.
- The map/place/airport context.
- The record or receipt location.
- Whether the app is using live data, cached data, or needs setup.
